For this course you will have to make a project and present it in the final weeks.

Goal

The goal of the project is to show me that you have a solid understanding of the basics of Machine Learning. What I would like to see is that you are able of:

Define a task to solve and select appropriate datasets that help you solve it
Analyze and process a dataset
Choose the appropriate model and know the strengths and weaknesses of the models you select
Train, validate and measure the performance of your models
Bonus if you have models with different hyperparameters and you run validation or cross-validation to choose them
Analyze the results of your trained model: analyze the errors of your model, know whether they are under/overfitting and how to correct that, see for which data samples and try to get why it is failing there
Interpret your results in the context of the task that you wanted to achieve
Instructions

Updated model-count clarification provided by the student on 14 October 2026:
- 4 simple models; or
- 3 models: 2 simple + 1 complex (such as an ANN, CNN, or transformer); or
- 2 models: 1 simple + 1 very complex.
The instructor's distinction between complex and very complex has not yet been specified. The original brief below is preserved for reference; use the updated model-count clarification for planning.

You will have to select a task that you want to do (the one you like the most, really). Then, select a dataset that can help you do that task. Select either: 4 basic ML models (k-NN, linear regression, logistic regression, naive Bayes, decision trees, tree ensembles) or 2 basic models and a deep ML model (deep neural networks or convolutional neural networks and maybe n-gram models with bag of words or TF-IDF count as difficult too). After selecting a dataset and a model, just try to do what I stated in the Goals section: do the project end-to-end.

Deliverables

By the last week of November you will have to send me the code that you used in your project. 
In the last week of November you will also make a 15 minutes oral presentation in the classroom.
